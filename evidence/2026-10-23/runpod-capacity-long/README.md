# Longer-window 128-client capacity cohort

**Completed: 10 valid windows; six pass the registered stability criteria.** Under one fixed source/profile, 13k offered commands/s passes both 14.615-second rounds, delivering 12,992.816–12,992.953 accepted confirmations/s with 36.129–38.000 ms scheduled-to-confirmed p99. The two 14k rounds are mixed: one narrowly fails drain and one passes. Both 15k rounds fail stability, with 14,355.395–14,650.421 accepted confirmations/s, 422.245–577.398 ms scheduled p99 and 516.951–596.226 ms drain; the second round has 284 client-FIFO drops, so its p99 is conditional. At 16k for 11.875 seconds, goodput is 14,635.116/s with 7,932 FIFO drops and 596.326 ms conditional p99. All rounds, including failed criteria and export recovery, remain visible.

The useful result is a repeated passing finite-window rate of 13k/s, a variable 14k boundary, and repeated instability at 15k/s for this 128-client replicated path. High-load goodput is approximately 14.36–14.65k/s while all synchronous slots are nearly continuously occupied. This does not isolate the bare matching engine or establish indefinite capacity. Across 1,782,000 raw scheduled arrivals, 1,773,784 are finally accepted, 8,216 are explicitly never sent because their client FIFO is full, and initial unknowns, unresolved outcomes, business rejections and server errors are zero. Business completion is not a packet-loss-free network claim: transport counters show repaired receive errors.

This is a separate, larger bounded-fixture cohort. The preceding 90k cohort tracked 12k offered commands/s for only 7.5 seconds and showed overload at 16k for 5.625 seconds. Those short windows do not establish the >=10-second boundary used here; old and new configurations are not pooled.

## Per-arrival and resource audit

For each completed run, recompute goodput from all raw arrivals: count `CONFIRMED_ACCEPTED` rows whose confirmation offset is inside the arrival window, then divide by that window. Keep confirmations during drain separate. Scheduled-to-confirmed latency includes scheduler and FIFO delay. Queue-full requests were never sent; a timeout is unknown until resolved with the identical business identity. The worker does not advance its producer past an unresolved identity.

For slot occupancy, intersect each worker's execution-through-resolution interval with the arrival window and sum the busy durations. Divide by the window to obtain average occupied slots and by 128 for utilization. FIFO slots are waiting capacity, not additional in-flight service requests. Compare per-worker distributions to detect uneven assignment. `128 / mean API execute` is an accounting-derived hypothetical window ceiling using the same observed RTT, not an independently measured server capacity; it cannot justify extrapolation when RTT changes under load.

Where any offered arrival is dropped, rejected, errored or unresolved, a low conditional p99 cannot establish the accepted-command p99 of all offered arrivals. The all-offered p99 must remain unknown/null. Preserve every raw row and every failed or invalid round.

Process CPU cores must be recomputed from verified process user+system tick deltas and monotonic elapsed seconds, restricted to measurement-window observations. Record cgroup-throttling increments rather than cumulative historical totals. Private-interface traffic, namespace UDP receive/send errors and official CnC NAK/retransmit/backpressure counters can support explanations, but cannot alone prove a specific socket, unique packet loss, matching-core bottleneck or durable-storage latency. The old 16k point's receive errors are prior-cohort evidence, not an assumed outcome here. Shared load generation, embedded drivers, individual hot threads, GC and scheduling remain possible contributors even when total quota is not exhausted.

## What this establishes

Both 13k windows pass; one of two 14k windows passes; neither 15k window passes; the single 16k window loses client admission. No round is removed. The highest repeated rate that passes every retained round is 13k/s for 14.615-second windows. A 14k production capacity guarantee would be unsupported, and there is no repeated loss-free 15k result. The observed high-load accepted-window plateau, approximately 14.36–14.65k/s, measures this exact 128-in-flight client + replicated matching deployment. At 15k/16k, slot occupancy exceeds 99.9%; distinguishing a larger client window from cluster, embedded-driver, hot-thread, shared-host or network limits would require a separately identified experiment.
